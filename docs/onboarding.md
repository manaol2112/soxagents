# Engagement onboarding

Every new engagement opens in **Setup**. A team sets it up once, the planner drafts what it can for them to confirm, and then proposes the work. Approving the plan launches the engagement.

## Creating an engagement

**New engagement** on the Engagements page asks for the client, the engagement's name, the kind of engagement (SOX IT general controls, SOX ITGC and business process, or IT audit), the period end if known, and the engagement lead. The slug comes from the name. The profile is kept on the engagement's settings (`client_name`, `engagement_type`, migration 0039) and can be changed in the Engagement profile step. The engagement opens as a draft on the setup overview, and the planner is asked to draft its setup at once ([Agent-drafted setup](#agent-drafted-setup)).

## Steps

Setup opens on an **overview**: where each step stands, what the agents do, and what happens at launch. The four work steps can be taken in any order; the rail on the left shows which are ready. Old links to the former steps (`scope`, `rcm`, `workpapers`, `pbc`, `start`) open the step that replaced them.

| Step | What the team sets up | Ready when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Engagement profile | Client, methodology, period start and end, fiscal year, reporting objectives, and the scope narrative; the planner's draft of the scope and period to accept or edit | Scope, period end, and methodology are set, and the period agrees with the engagement's name and scope or a person confirmed it ([Profile and period](#profile-and-period)) |
| Team | An engagement lead, preparers, an independent reviewer, as three role cards | There is a lead and a reviewer |
| Controls and systems | The client's RCM (its in-scope systems are identified from it) or the standard library; the in-scope systems, each scoped with its controls (IT general controls, application controls, and key reports are listed under the system they run on, and open in the control editor from there); the business process controls listed by cycle | At least one control is in scope, there is at least one in-scope system, and each IT control has a system. Control owners are not assigned on setup: a request without one goes to the PBC coordinator |
| Workpaper templates | One workbook template per kind of control (IT general controls, application controls, business process controls, key reports), each with design, interim testing, and roll-forward tabs; the firm's own, customised from a standard one in the editor or uploaded; each previewed as a workbook with a real control, applied to every control of its kind, and confirmed | A person confirmed the templates |
| Launch | The engagement memo (the setup as it will start, each line opening its step); right under it the launch decision, with the Launch engagement button, or what is still missing and a link to it; then readiness, the agents, and what launching creates | The plan is approved |

Readiness is computed from the records each time. It is never stored, so it cannot go stale. Each item has **issues**, which hold launch, and **advisories**, which are worth knowing and do not; the launch step lists both, apart (what to fix before launch, and what is worth knowing). The API's readiness items keep their keys (`scope`, `team`, `rcm`, `workpapers`, `pbc`, `agents`); the page folds `rcm` and `workpapers` into the Controls and systems step.

## Profile and period

Migration 0074. The profile records how the engagement is run, on `engagement_settings`: the **methodology** (`EXTERNAL_ICFR`, external audit of ICFR under PCAOB AS 2201, integrated; `MANAGEMENT_404`, management's SOX 404 testing; `INTERNAL_AUDIT`, internal audit or IT audit), the **period start**, the **fiscal year** label, and the **reporting objectives**. All are set in the Engagement profile step (`PATCH …/onboarding/profile`; a field left out is kept, while an explicit `null` clears an optional date before launch). The methodology is required before launch. Every methodology requires an independent reviewer; nobody reviews their own work. A period that starts on or after it ends is refused.

**The period check** (`profile_checks.py`). The period end is a record; the name, the scope narrative, and the fiscal year label are words. When they disagree, the page says so and launch waits:

- the engagement's name names another fiscal year ("2027 SOX Audit" with a period ending 31 December 2026);
- the scope names the period end's day and month in another year ("through December 31, 2027"), or another fiscal year ("FY2027"); an interim date is not a period end and is not flagged;
- the fiscal year label names another year;
- the period starts on or after it ends.

A period that is not about twelve months long is said as an advisory (a short or transition period is allowed). Nothing changes a date on its own: a person corrects the name, scope, or dates, or **confirms the period as recorded** (`POST …/onboarding/period/confirm` with the `basis` the page showed). The confirmation holds only for what it was checked against: any change to the name, scope, start, end, or fiscal year asks again. It is in the audit trail (`onboarding.period_confirmed`). The onboarding body carries `period` (its conflicts, `basis`, and whether and by whom it was confirmed).

**Engagements already running.** An engagement launched before these checks keeps its period. Its dashboard shows the disagreement (`period_conflicts` on the dashboard) with a link to the profile, where a person can confirm the period as recorded; the profile itself is locked after launch. Correcting a running engagement's period is a later change.

**The planner** never keeps a scope narrative that names another period than the one it comes with: the reviewed rules write the scope instead, and the draft says why (`setup_drafting._checked`).

## Agent-drafted setup

Milestone 8.3c. The engagement planner drafts the setup from the engagement's own records (`setup_drafting.py`, table `setup_drafts`): a scope narrative, the period end, and the evidence-request cadence, each with a one-line reason. It runs when an engagement is created and whenever a person asks (**Ask the planner** or **Draft again**), as a background job like the other drafts (the local worker or the `setup_draft` workflow, with retries and a resume on restart). It never runs once the engagement has started.

- With a model, Claude writes the narrative and weighs the cadence from the engagement's name, client, kind, period, controls by area, systems, and owners. The answer is checked: the scope is cleaned and refused if it holds a secret, a period end outside three years of today falls back to the rules, a period end already set is kept, and the cadence must be within the allowed ranges.
- Without a model, or when the answer does not fit, the reviewed rules in `setup_suggest.py` draft it: the fiscal year in the engagement's name gives the period end (a calendar year end is assumed), the number of controls sets the cadence, and the scope is written from the client, the kind, the systems, and the control areas.

Nothing is applied on its own. Each suggestion is shown next to the field it is for, with its reason, and a person accepts the ticked ones (`POST .../onboarding/suggestions/accept`, against the draft's version) or edits the field instead. Accepting the scope writes the engagement's scope; accepting the period end or the cadence writes the settings. The draft, each acceptance, and the profile are in the audit trail.

**Owner emails.** When every owner email already entered follows one pattern on one of the client's domains (`first.last`, `flast`, and so on), the same pattern is suggested for owners the RCM names without an email, shown on the Evidence requests step with the controls each owns. A person confirms them through the existing owner-emails form; nothing is applied on its own.

## Team and access

The team is the engagement's project membership, so being on it is what gives a project manager, reviewer, or contributor access to the engagement. Owners and admins can open every engagement. The engagement role (lead, preparer, reviewer) is descriptive; the organization role still decides permissions.

**Removing someone from a running engagement** says what it does first (`GET …/onboarding/team/{user}/impact`): their open tasks go back to the team, unassigned (`released_tasks` in the audit), and the work they did keeps their name. The only independent reviewer of a running engagement cannot be removed while independence is enforced ("name another reviewer first").

**Development mode.** `REVIEW_INDEPENDENCE_REQUIRED=false` lets anyone on the team act as the reviewer, for development only. Readiness says so as an advisory, the Team step shows a banner, the API logs a warning at start, and it refuses to start with `APP_ENV=production`.

### In-scope systems first

The Controls and systems step starts with the in-scope systems. The standard library stays locked until there is at least one, because every control is one instance per system (the API refuses `controls/load-library` with "add an in-scope system first"); uploading the client RCM is open from the start, since it finds the systems itself. Systems can be typed in, or ticked from **Choose from common systems**: about 58 well-known applications, databases, operating systems, infrastructure, and tools from the reviewed catalog in `rcm_systems.py` (`POST …/onboarding/applications/known`). Each keeps its catalog layer, only catalog names are accepted there, systems already in scope are not offered again, and plain "SAP" is left out in favour of the specific products.

### Scoping each system

Each in-scope system is scoped on its own, through the **system wizard** (Milestone 8.10; `system_applicability.py`, `system_scoping.py`, migration 0065). **Add system** in the systems panel of the Controls and systems step opens it; **Review controls** on an open system opens it again at its controls. Nothing asks up front how the system is hosted or whether it has a SOC report.

1. **System.** Name and kind (from the catalog or typed), then a description, its business purpose, the processes it supports, and its owner (`PATCH …/onboarding/systems/{id}/details`). The system is created when this step is saved; cancelling the wizard removes a system it created.

   **Suggest details** (`POST …/onboarding/systems/suggest`, `system_suggestions.py`) asks the agent to suggest these fields. It works from what is known of the named product and from the engagement's own records: the controls that run on the system or name it, their processes, and their recorded owners. The client name and the other in-scope systems are context only. Nothing is saved. The wizard fills only the empty fields, leaves the kind alone once a person has chosen it, and marks each filled field with where its value came from: the engagement's records, the reviewed catalogue, or what is known of the product. The mark clears when a person edits the field.

   The agent cannot invent client facts. An owner is either a role title or a name copied from the records; any other name, and any email address, is dropped. A value holding a credential is dropped. A product the agent does not recognise gets no description, purpose, or owner. The team's kind and the reviewed catalogue's kind take precedence over the agent's. Without a model, or when the answer does not fit the schema, only the catalogue's kind and the processes recorded on the engagement are suggested, and the wizard says so. Asking needs the setup rights that editing the details needs, and each model call is recorded.
2. **Controls.** The candidate controls, grouped by area: the library's controls, or the client's own controls when the RCM is the source (the library is never offered next to an upload). The **magic wand** (`POST …/onboarding/systems/{id}/review`) asks the scoping agent to assess the system. It marks each candidate **Recommended**, **Potentially applicable**, **Not recommended**, or **Needs your judgment**, with its rationale, what it rests on, what is still missing, and its confidence. Selecting a control is always the team's choice; **Select all recommended** is a shortcut, not a default.
3. **Review.** The system overview with the agent's assumptions and open questions, the selected controls, the SOC 1 assessment and any report's CUECs, and a reason for each recommended control left out. **Save selection** (`PUT …/onboarding/systems/{id}/applicability`) is the only thing that changes scope.

**What the agent may do.** The agent reads the system's details and the candidate controls. It may search the web (Anthropic's web search tool, at most three searches; its instructions limit queries to the product or vendor name) to learn whether the vendor states that it issues a SOC 1 report. It may cite only pages the search returned; a statement without such a source is downgraded to "not researched". Its answer is checked before it is kept: a control it names must be a candidate, an unknown one is dropped, and one it leaves out is marked Needs your judgment. Without a configured model, reviewed rules stand in and say so (access controls recommended, the reviewed set per kind of system in `GENERAL_BY_LAYER` potentially applicable, the rest left to the team's judgment, and the SOC 1 assessment Undetermined). Reviews run only when the wand is used or a SOC report is uploaded, never on their own, so no web search is made that nobody asked for.

**SOC 1.** The assessment says whether the system relies on a service organization whose services matter to financial reporting: **Relevant**, **Not currently identified as relevant**, or **Undetermined** when the evidence is not enough. A SOC 2 report is not a substitute: an uploaded report that is not a SOC 1 says so on the report. The open system shows the assessment, the vendor, the basis, and the sources it cites, and offers **Upload the SOC 1 report** (`POST …/onboarding/systems/{id}/soc-reports/upload`, PDF or text, up to 25 MB). The agent reads an uploaded report in the background: its kind, type, period, opinion (with its page), scope, control objectives, exceptions, subservice organizations, and its complementary user entity controls (CUECs). A finding or CUEC is kept only when words copied from it (at least 20 characters) are found in the report's text, and its page is the page those words were found on, never the page the model claims. Each CUEC is mapped to the controls that can answer it (one CUEC to several controls, and one control to several CUECs) and shown as **Mapped to a control in scope**, **Partly mapped**, **No control in scope**, or **Not yet assessed**. A mapped control the team has not put in scope does not count. A report with no readable text (a scan) is marked Not read. Nothing here is taken from what such reports usually say: the generic CUECs and SOC coverage of earlier milestones were removed, and migration 0065 cleared what they had stored. **Remove** on a report (`POST …/onboarding/soc-reports/{id}/remove`) deletes it and its CUECs.

**Recommendations and decisions are kept apart.** `system_control_assessments` holds, per system and control, the agent's status, source (agent or rules), rationale, support, missing information, and confidence, next to the team's decision (selected or not), its note, who made it, and when. A new assessment updates the recommendation and keeps the decision. Leaving out a Recommended control needs a reason; the API refuses the save without one. The audit trail records `onboarding.system_details`, `onboarding.system_assessed`, `onboarding.system_applicability` (with the selected and not-selected controls and those against the recommendation), `onboarding.soc_report_uploaded`, and `onboarding.soc_report_removed`.

A system's row shows its hosting as assessed and its SOC 1 standing. A system the agent assessed and the team has not saved yet shows **Review controls**; a system with no control in scope shows "No controls", since nothing would be tested for it. The open system also lists **Controls in scope** for it, however they came in, each with Take out (`POST …/onboarding/systems/{id}/controls/remove`).

The Controls step lists **SOC 1 reports to address**: every system whose SOC 1 assessment is Relevant or Undetermined, or that has a report uploaded, with the CUECs no in-scope control answers. It is a list for the client to consider (the period covered and any gap, the opinion and exceptions, and the CUECs; KPMG ICFR Handbook (2025) chapter 8); nothing there is tracked or decided. Reviewing a vendor's SOC 1 report is an entity-level control, covered with the ELCs.

### Inviting by email

Whoever can change the team (an owner, an admin, or a project manager on the engagement) can invite someone by email from the Team step, with a role (`POST …/onboarding/team/invite`, `invitations.py`, migration 0045). The person joins the firm and the team in one step, and the role applies at once. Their firm role follows the engagement role: a lead joins as project manager, a preparer as contributor, a reviewer as reviewer. An invitation never grants owner or admin. Someone already in the firm is just added to the team.

An address is refused, and nobody is created, when it is:

- not a plain email address (one `@`, a valid local part and domain, at most 254 characters);
- not on one of the firm's own email domains;
- on a known temporary-mail domain;
- on a domain with no mail servers (a DNS MX lookup, falling back to an address record; a "null MX" counts as none). If the lookup cannot finish, the invite is refused with "try again" rather than let through;
- or the inviter has already sent 20 invitations in the last hour.

The firm's email domains are listed on the Team step (`/organizations/{id}/email-domains`). Every member can see them; owners and admins add and remove them. A public mail service such as Gmail, a temporary-mail domain, or a domain with no mail servers cannot be a firm domain. A firm made by Google or Microsoft sign-up starts with its creator's work domain; migration 0045 seeded each existing firm with its owners' work domains.

Each team member shows **Validated** once they have signed in with that address (Google or Microsoft verified it is theirs) and **Pending validation** until then. Their role works either way.

The welcome email (who invited them, the firm, the engagement, the role, and how to sign in) is written and stored with the invitation. Live email is off, so it is held, and the team step shows "Welcome email ready". Nothing is sent until a later milestone turns sending on.

## RCM

The control library is code in `services/api/ai_workforce_api/controls.py`: 48 controls in 14 areas. There are 16 IT general controls (logical access, change management, IT operations), application controls and key reports (IPE), and starter controls for entity-level controls and each business cycle (order to cash, procure to pay, record to report, treasury, hire to retire, inventory, fixed assets, tax). Each has a risk, a control activity, test attributes, suggested evidence, and a default frequency and nature. A test pins the library's fingerprint. The team chooses which areas to load, and can add areas later. Loading copies the controls into the engagement, which keeps the library key and version. Sample sizes follow frequency (annual 1, quarterly and monthly 2, weekly 5, daily 20, more often or event-driven 25). An automated control is tested once.

### Controls per application (8.5)

A control that runs on several systems is one engagement control per system (`engagement_controls.engagement_application_id`, migration 0041), with the same control ID: the *instances* of the control, named "ITGC-AC-01 · Active Directory" and "ITGC-AC-01 · Oracle". Loading the library or importing an RCM creates one instance per in-scope system a control runs on; a control with no system has one row with none. The setup shows one row per control ID and edits the family: ticking a system adds an instance, unticking one removes it (unless it already has requests, testing, or findings), and every other field is written to each instance. Adding a system to the engagement gives every control an instance for it; removing one takes its instances away. After kickoff, each instance has its own evidence request, test plan and sheet, conclusion, workpapers, and deficiencies, so one application can be effective while another has an exception. The control library's `control_instances.py` keeps families in step, and existing engagements were split by the migration.

### Business process controls belong to a process

Only controls that run on a system (the three ITGC areas, application controls, and key reports: `SYSTEM_DOMAINS` in `controls.py`) are one instance per system. Entity-level controls and the business cycles belong to a process: one row each, with no system (migration 0054 collapsed the per-system copies in engagements not yet started; a copy with requests, testing, or findings was kept). Loading the library creates them once, with or without a system, so a library load of business areas alone needs no system; an RCM import gives them one row whatever systems the RCM names; a new system joins only the families that run on systems; the control editor offers no systems for them; and readiness asks for systems only for controls that run on one.

A team can also **name a process of the client's own** when the standard cycles do not describe it ("Lease accounting", "Royalties"): **Add a process** on the Business process tab (`POST …/onboarding/processes` with a name and the standard cycle it is based on; table `engagement_processes`, migration 0059). The cycle it is based on decides the tester, the sample sizes, and the workpaper template of its controls. A named process is a row like the cycles, with the controls the team writes for it (`engagement_controls.process_id`); each keeps the process's name as its own word for its area, so the register and the workpapers show the process. A named process with no controls can be removed (`POST …/onboarding/processes/{id}/remove`); one with controls stays, and its controls are taken out of scope instead. Both work before and after launch, like adding a system.

On the Controls and systems step each tab scopes its own kind: the IT controls tab shows the in-scope systems, the Business process tab shows each process as a row (its controls in scope, the ones taken out, and the library's starter controls for it: `POST …/onboarding/controls/add` with a library key adds one without a system, `POST …/onboarding/controls/{id}/scope` puts one in or out), and the Key reports tab shows each report as a row with the systems it is tested for (it can be added for another in-scope system: `POST …/onboarding/systems/{id}/controls/add` with another system's instance copies it for that system).

## Areas and testers

Every control belongs to one of 15 standard areas: the three IT general control areas, application controls (ITAC), key reports and IPE, entity-level controls, the eight business cycles, and other business process. The area decides the standard tester: the three ITGC testers take their own areas, and the business process controls tester takes everything else. A person can change a control's area, or choose another tester for one control (for example, an ITGC tester for an application control). An uploaded RCM keeps the client's own word for the area next to the standard one.

## Client RCM upload

Instead of the library, a team can upload the client's own RCM, in the client's format and terminology. The library and an upload are alternatives: either one replaces the other until kickoff.

1. **Upload.** An `.xlsx` workbook or a `.csv` file, up to 5 MB, 20 sheets, 2,000 rows, and 80 columns. The file is untrusted. Only cell values are read: formulas are not evaluated (a workbook's cached values are used), and macros, links, and embedded objects are ignored. Workbooks are opened through `defusedxml` and checked against zip bombs. The file is not stored; the import keeps its hash, size, and parsed cells. A file name is a label only.
2. **Layout.** The sheet and heading row are detected (a cover sheet or title rows are skipped) and can be changed.
3. **Columns.** Each client column is matched to a platform field by its heading and by its values together: a heading names a field, the values confirm it, veto it, or choose between two columns ("Risk ID" holds identifiers, so "Risk (What Could Go Wrong)" is the risk; a "Nature" column holding preventive and detective is the control's type, kept as a client column, and the column holding manual and automated is the nature). Frequency, nature, and key control can be named by their values alone. The process or area can come from two columns read together, a category (ITGC, Business Process, Key Report) and the process itself: the term is shown as "category / process", and a category narrows the process to its kind of area, so "ITGC / Vendor management" is IT operations, not procure to pay, while ITAC, key report, and entity-level categories decide outright. Each client column is matched to a platform field (control ID, name, description, risk, process or area, frequency, nature, key control, owner, owner email, applications, test procedures, evidence), kept as a client column, or dropped. Suggestions come from a reviewed synonym list in `services/api/ai_workforce_api/rcm_import.py`, and first from how this engagement's own earlier uploads were mapped. An engagement never learns from another engagement's RCM: headings and client terms are client data. A person confirms the mapping. Control ID and description are required.
4. **Terms.** For process or area, frequency, and nature, every distinct client term is mapped once to a standard value, because the standard value decides the tester agent, the sample size, and the testing template. At upload, reviewed keyword rules and this engagement's earlier uploads suggest values ("As needed" to event-driven, "ITDM" to IT-dependent manual). Then, in the background, an agent maps the terms that are still blank: Claude reads each term with the controls that use it (so "IT" next to access reviews is logical access) and gives a one-line reason, shown under the term. **Remap with agent** redoes every term and replaces the current choices; without a model it puts back the rule suggestions. The answer is checked: only terms in the file and only standard values are taken. Changing which column holds a term maps again. Nothing imports while a term is unmapped. Rows without a control ID add no terms.
5. **In-scope systems.** In the background, an agent identifies every system the RCM relies on: from the systems column, and from the control text, which often names systems the column leaves out (the ticketing tool used for approvals, the directory used for access, the database behind a backup control). It merges the ways one system is written ("SAP", "SAP ECC 6.0") under one name, sets its layer (application, database, operating system, infrastructure, or tool), and links it to the controls that rely on it. Claude does this when a model is configured; its answer is kept only where a system's name or a variant appears in the RCM text and its control IDs exist, so it cannot add systems the RCM does not name. Without a model, the systems column and a reviewed list of common systems in `services/api/ai_workforce_api/rcm_systems.py` are used. A person renames, re-layers, or leaves out systems before import. Changing the column mapping scans again. Values like "N/A" or "All systems" are not systems.
6. **Preview and import.** The preview lists the controls, the rows that will be skipped and why (no control ID, a repeated ID, no description, a credential-shaped value), and applications that will be added. Importing replaces the engagement's controls in one transaction, adds the confirmed systems as in-scope applications (each control tagged with its own), adds any other new applications, and asks for the workpaper templates to be confirmed again. An RCM kept on several sheets (business process controls on one, ITGCs on another) is imported sheet by sheet: the import reads one sheet at a time, and once an upload is the source the import offers **Add to the controls already imported** (`mode: ADD`; a control with the same ID is replaced by its new row, library controls still give way) next to **Replace**.

Each imported control keeps the client's words for area, frequency, and nature next to the standard value, and every kept client column under its own heading. Both appear on the control and on its workpapers. When the RCM names owners without emails, the team adds each owner's email once and every control they own gets it. A control ID becomes a PBC reference by replacing characters that references do not allow (`AC 1.01` becomes `AC-1.01`).

### Controls the team writes

When neither the library nor the client's RCM has a control, the team writes it on the Controls step (`POST …/onboarding/controls/new`): **New business process control** at the foot of the Business process tab (it offers every standard cycle and every process the team named, so no process has to exist first), **New … control** inside a process row, **New key report** on the Key reports card. The form takes the control ID, title, risk, activity, area, frequency, nature, whether it is key, the test attributes, and the evidence to request. A key report belongs to the system it comes from; a business process control to its cycle. The control is added in scope with its area's tester and the standard template for its kind (`source = 'MANUAL'`, migration 0058); a repeated ID is refused. After launch it gets its work at once, like any control that joins scope.

## Workpaper templates

Workpaper templates are their own setup step, after Controls and systems. A template is a **workbook**: a design tab, an interim testing tab, and a roll-forward tab, each with its sections, so a control is documented in one workpaper rather than a design workpaper and a testing workpaper. Each kind of control has its own standard workbook, code in `services/api/ai_workforce_api/workpapers.py`, with the sections that kind calls for:

- **IT general control** (`itgc`, areas ACCESS, CHANGE, OPERATIONS): understanding of the control in the system, a walkthrough, the system listings relied on, design attributes; population, sample, testing matrix, exceptions; changes since interim, an update sample, roll-forward testing.
- **Application control** (`itac`): understanding of the configuration, a walkthrough, reliance on IT general controls; configuration inspected and a test of one; configuration changes since interim and a roll-forward test.
- **Business process control** (`business`, every business cycle): process narrative, walkthrough, segregation of duties, information produced by the entity; population, sample, matrix, exceptions; changes since interim and an update sample.
- **Key report** (`key-report`, area IPE): report description, source data and logic, reliance on IT general controls; the report instance tested, completeness and accuracy procedures (starter rows for completeness, accuracy, parameters, and logic), a matrix; changes to the report since interim.

A control keeps one template key (`engagement_controls.template_key`, migration 0056), the standard one for its area unless the team chooses another of the same kind; a control that moves to another area takes that area's template. **Preview** opens a dialog over the step laid out like a spreadsheet: the open tab's sections above, the sheet tabs along the bottom (Escape, the backdrop, or the close button closes it; the tab buttons and the control picker switch without reloading the page). A preview fills every tab with one engagement control: its header fields, the sample size for the interim and the update sample for the roll-forward, and a testing matrix with one row per sample and one column per attribute. Previews never contain results. Changing a control's template asks for confirmation again.

A firm keeps its own templates per engagement (`workpaper_templates.py`, migration 0055), two ways:

- **Customise a standard one.** **Customise** on any template copies it as the firm's own (`POST …/onboarding/workpaper-templates/customize` with the base key), and **Edit** opens the editor: rename the template and its tabs, change what a tab covers, rename sections, change the guidance the preparer sees and the columns of a table, add starter rows, and add, reorder, or remove sections and tabs. **Save as your firm's template** posts the whole template as one JSON field, checked on the web side and again by the API (`PUT …/workpaper-templates/{id}`, against the version it was based on). Header fields, the testing matrix, and the sign-off keep being filled from the records; a narrative is drafted by the agent and completed by a person.
- **Upload the firm's workpaper.** A Word document or an Excel workbook, for one kind of control (`POST …/onboarding/workpaper-templates?file_name=&kind=&part=`). Sheets, or short top headings, named Design, Interim, or Roll-forward become the tabs; under each, headings become sections, the text under a heading becomes that section's guidance, a two-column table of labels becomes the header fields, a table whose heading or columns mention attributes or samples becomes the testing matrix, a sign-off table becomes the sign-off, and any other table keeps its columns (rows with only their first cell filled become its starter rows). A file that covers one tab (`part` design, interim, or rollforward) becomes that tab; the standard tabs of the kind fill in whatever the file does not cover, so the workbook is always complete. A whole-workbook upload with no recognisable tabs is refused with the reason.

A firm template appears under its kind with the file name or the standard template it started from, can be previewed with a real control, and **Use for all <kind>** points every control of that kind at it (`…/workpaper-templates/{id}/apply`); the control editor can also choose it per control. Previews, the testers' workpapers, and the agent's drafting then follow the firm's tabs and sections, and the agent drafts every narrative section (an effectiveness conclusion is always derived from testing). Removing a template (`…/remove`) puts the standard template back on the controls that used it. Uploading, customising, saving, applying, or removing a template asks the team to confirm the templates again. An uploaded file is kept as it was; filling that file itself with results is not done: the workpaper is produced in the platform's own format, following the template's tabs and sections.

## Kickoff

When every step is ready, the launch page shows the plan as it stands (`plan_preview` on the onboarding body): for each in-scope control a walkthrough, an evidence request, an operating effectiveness test (after the walkthrough and the evidence), and an independent review (after the test), ending with the engagement report after every review. Each task goes to the right standard agent. The plan is a function of the setup, so there is nothing to propose or edit; the page shows what launching creates and one button, **Launch engagement**, which records the plan and approves it in one step (`POST …/plan/launch` with the fingerprint the page showed). The older two-step route, propose (`POST …/plan`) then approve, still exists.

Launching, in one transaction:

- Locks the profile, the RCM source (library load and RCM upload), and the request plan. The team, the scope (systems and controls), and the workpaper templates can still change; see [Changing scope after launch](#changing-scope-after-launch) and [Workpaper templates after launch](#workpaper-templates-after-launch).
- Activates the engagement.
- Creates every task as ready, assigned to its agent, with its dependencies.
- Writes each control's evidence plan from the reviewed rules and leaves it proposed. Nothing is requested until a person approves that plan. Design evidence is drafted on approval; operating evidence also waits until the control's attributes are settled.

The plan is fingerprinted against the complete launch-relevant snapshot: the engagement profile and period, team, applications, in-scope controls, selected workpaper templates, agents, and generated tasks. If any of that setup changes after the page showed it, the launch is refused and the page shows the updated plan. An engagement starts once: the kickoff is claimed atomically, so a second launch (another tab, a double press) is refused with "engagement has started" and the page says it has already launched. The launch button sends one request per press. On the launch step the page header offers no button of its own: the launch is under the memo, and a header link to the same page did nothing. Once launched, the setup page leads to the dashboard and the launch step shows where the work stands; it never offers the launch again. The page shows:

- when the engagement launched and who launched it;
- its scope (controls, systems, team, agent tasks);
- three progress cards: evidence plans approved, evidence requests by stage (drafted, awaiting approval, with the client, received), and agent tasks done;
- the next steps, most pressing first: approve the evidence plans, plan any unplanned controls, approve and review requests, attend to blocked or failed tasks, and whether the planner is still working;
- the work plan the engagement launched with, by control.

The figures come from `launch` in the setup snapshot (`LaunchSummary`, counted from the records on each read, never stored). Nothing is emailed: each request still needs an approver before it is sent, and no evidence is requested before its control's plan is approved (see [The planning gate](pbc.md#the-planning-gate)). See [Drafting from the control](pbc.md#drafting-from-the-control).

Owners, admins, and project managers on the engagement change setup and approve the plan. Everyone with access can follow it.

## Workpaper templates after launch

Milestone 8.16 (migration 0069). The Workpaper templates step stays open after launch for the people who run the engagement: a standard template can be customised, a firm template edited, copied, applied, or removed, and the firm's own workpaper uploaded, as before launch (`…/workpaper-templates/…` accepts them after launch).

A change reaches only workpapers that have not started. A workpaper starts the first time a person or the agent writes in it, or when it is prepared; one filled only from the records has not started, since it is filled again from the records in any shape (`workpapers.started_at`). When it starts it keeps a copy of the template it began with (`workpapers.template_snapshot`), and it is drafted in that template from then on, even if the firm's template is edited or removed. A workpaper that has not started follows its control's template: when a template changes, is applied, or is removed, the workpapers not started that use it take the control's template and are drafted again in its shape (`workpaper_templates.follow_template`). Changing a control's template on the control does the same.

Confirming the templates is a step before launch. After launch nothing asks for it again, so the step does not show work to do; a change before launch still asks for it. Migration 0069 restores the confirmation for engagements where a change after launch had cleared it, to the time they launched, since launching required it.

## Changing scope after launch

Systems and controls keep changing once an engagement is running, so the Controls and systems step stays open after launch for the people who run the engagement, with the same pages as before: add a system and scope it with the system wizard, add a control from the library or to a system, edit a control, and put a control in or out of scope. Loading the standard library again and uploading an RCM are not offered after launch, since each replaces the whole register.

Each change is reconciled with the running work at once (`scope_changes.py`), so the register and the engagement never disagree:

- A control that comes into scope (added, included again, or an instance a new system brings) gets the work the kickoff plan gives every control: a walkthrough, an evidence request, a test, and an independent review, each assigned to its agent in dependency order, with its review feeding the engagement report. Its evidence request is drafted by the evidence coordinator in the background and sent only once an approver confirms it, as at kickoff.
- A control that leaves scope has its open tasks cancelled ("Taken out of scope") and its unsent evidence requests cancelled. Testing, workpapers, and requests already sent are kept.
- Removing a system after launch keeps the system on record and takes its controls out of scope, with their open work cancelled, so what was tested stays attributable to it. Before launch a system is deleted as before. Either way the page asks first and says how many of its controls are in scope and what happens to them.

**One of each (Milestone 8.13).** A system is in scope once, however its name is written: "SAP ERP" and " sap  erp" are the same system. Adding it again, by name, from the common systems, or in the system wizard, is refused with "system is already in scope", and the wizard says so before it calls the API; cancelling the wizard removes only a system that the wizard itself added. A control ID is likewise one control per system however it is cased ("control ID exists"). The database holds both rules (migration 0067: unique indexes on the folded system name and the upper-cased control ID).

**Changing a tested control.** A control with an approved sample or a test sheet (on any of its systems) is marked `tested` on the onboarding body, and its editor says so. A change to its frequency, nature, or area, which its sample size, attributes, and tester rest on, is refused with "control has testing on record" unless the request says `acknowledge_testing: true`. When it goes ahead, each tested instance gets an open question, *Changed after testing: …*, which holds its testing until a person decides whether the sample and results still stand, and the audit event records `after_testing` with the fields that changed. Other edits (owner, key, in scope) are not affected.

**Impact first, and a reason where work is on record.** Taking a control or a system out of a running engagement shows what it affects before it is confirmed (`GET …/onboarding/scope-impact?control_id=…` or `?application_id=…`): open tasks cancelled, unsent requests withdrawn, and what is on record and kept (requests already sent, testing, workpapers, reviews, deficiencies). Where something is on record, the change needs a reason ("say why: this has work on record"), and the reason and the impact go in the audit event. This holds for the scope button, the control editor's in-scope box, a system's control list, and removing a system. Before launch no reason is asked. Excluding a control in the system wizard's applicability step and accepting reliance on a SOC report keep their own notes and do not ask for this reason yet.

Every change is in the audit trail (`scope.control_added`, `scope.control_removed`, `onboarding.application_out_of_scope`).
